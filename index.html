Types of Wine

Wine is fermented grape juice. Colour, sweetness, and effervescence come from how grapes are pressed, skin contact duration, and fermentation technique.

Red

Red Wine

Made with dark grape skins left during fermentation, adding rich color, tannins, and structural depth. Ranges from light (Pinot Noir) to full-bodied (Cabernet Sauvignon).

White

White Wine

Pressed prior to fermentation to remove skins. Typically crisp and fresh with vibrant acidity. Ranges from mineral (Sauvignon Blanc) to rich and buttery (oaked Chardonnay).

Rosé

Rosé Wine

Created from red grapes with brief skin contact (maceration), yielding an elegant pink hue. Expresses delicate strawberry, citrus, and floral notes with a dry finish.

Sparkling

Sparkling Wine

Effervescent wines with trapped carbon dioxide from secondary fermentation. Includes Champagne, Cava, Prosecco, and Crémant, each defined by regional rules.

Sweet

Dessert Wine

Luscious wines crafted from late-harvest, ice-frozen, or botrytised ('noble rot') grapes rich in natural sugars. Classic examples include Sauternes, Tokaji, and Ice Wine.

Fortified

Fortified Wine

Neutral grape spirits are added during or after fermentation to boost alcohol levels (17–22% ABV). Includes Port, Sherry, Madeira, and Marsala.

How to Taste Wine

Follow the classic 4-step sommelier sensory evaluation technique.

01

Look

Tilt glass against a white backdrop. Color intensity and rim variation reveal grape age and style—reds turn brick-garnet, whites deepen to gold.

02

Swirl & Smell

Gently swirl to aerate. Inhale aromas deeply: identify fruit layers, floral notes, spices, oak aging, or earthy undertones.

03

Sip

Coat your palate. Evaluate structural components: sweetness, tart acidity, drying tannins, alcohol warmth, and overall body.

04

Finish

Observe how long lingering flavors stay on your palate after swallowing. A complex, persistent finish signals exceptional quality.

Food Pairing

Balance weight with weight, and complement acidity or sweetness with dish flavor components.

Sauvignon Blanc

Goat cheese, fresh green salads, oysters, herb-driven seafood.

Chardonnay

Roast chicken, creamy fettuccine, butter-poached lobster, pork tenderloin.

Riesling

Spicy Thai/Vietnamese dishes, Peking duck, roasted pork, cured ham.

Pinot Noir

Pan-seared salmon, roast duck breast, wild mushroom risotto.

Cabernet Sauvignon

Prime ribeye steak, grilled lamb chops, aged cheddar or Gouda.

Sparkling

Crispy fried chicken, salty caviar, oysters, brunch pastries, tapas.

Sangiovese

Classic Neapolitan pizza, tomato-based ragù, grilled sausages.

Dessert Wine

Roquefort blue cheese, foie gras, lemon tart, fruit pavlova.

Serving & Storage Guidelines

Temperature control and proper glassware dramatically enhance wine aromas and flavors.

Wine Style Optimal Temperature Serving Note
Sparkling & Sweet Whites6–8 °C (43–46 °F)Well chilled; preserves bubbles & freshness.
Light Whites & Rosé8–10 °C (46–50 °F)Chilled; brings out vibrant fruit and acidity.
Full Whites & Light Reds10–14 °C (50-[57] °F)Slightly chilled; unveils oak & delicate aromas.
Medium to Full Reds15–18 °C (59–64 °F)Slightly below room temperature; softens tannin.

Cellar Storage

Store bottles horizontally at a steady 10–15 °C away from light and vibrations to keep corks moist.

Decanting

Young, tannic reds benefit from 30–60 minutes of aeration. Older vintage wines require gentle pouring off sediment.

Glassware Selection

Tulip-shaped glasses concentrate aromas. Fill one-third full to leave ample space for swirling.

Opened Bottles

Re-cork and refrigerate immediately. Whites last 3–5 days, reds 3–5 days, and sparkling 1–3 days with a pressure stopper.

Wine Glossary

Essential terminology for discussing flavor profiles, vinification, and wine quality.

Acidity
The tart, mouth-watering freshness that balances fruit and sugar structure.
Tannin
Phenolic compounds from grape skins, seeds, and oak providing a drying, structured mouthfeel.
Body
The tactile weight of wine in the mouth, categorized as light, medium, or full-bodied.
Terroir
The complete natural environment—soil type, climate, elevation, and terrain—shaping a wine's character.
Appellation
A legally defined, protected geographical indication for grape growing (e.g. AOC, DOCG, AVA).
Vintage
The specific calendar year in which the grapes were harvested.
Brut
A dry style of sparkling wine containing very low residual sugar levels (under 12g/L).
Corked (TCA)
A wine fault caused by 2,4,6-trichloroanisole, giving aromas of damp cardboard and muted fruit flavors.
