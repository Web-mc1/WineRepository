The Wine Guide

Everything you need to know about wine: styles, grapes, regions, tasting, food pairing, serving and the words people use.

Types of wine

Wine is fermented grape juice. Colour, sweetness and bubbles come from how the grapes are handled.

Red

Red wine

Made with the dark grape skins left in during fermentation, which adds colour, tannin and structure. Ranges from light (Pinot Noir) to full (Cabernet Sauvignon).

White

White wine

Usually pressed before fermentation, so skins are removed. Typically fresher, with higher perceived acidity. Ranges from crisp (Sauvignon Blanc) to rich (oaked Chardonnay).

Rosé

Rosé

Red grapes with brief skin contact, giving a pink colour. Dry, fruity and refreshing in most cases.

Sparkling

Sparkling

Carbon dioxide trapped in the bottle. Champagne, Cava, Prosecco and Crémant differ by region and method.

Sweet

Dessert wine

Sugar is left in the wine, often from late harvest, frozen or botrytised grapes. Examples: Sauternes, Ice wine, Tokaji.

Fortified

Fortified

Spirit is added to raise alcohol (about 17–22%). Examples: Port, Sherry, Madeira, Marsala.

How to taste wine

  1. Look

    Tilt the glass against white. Colour hints at age and grape. Reds fade to brick, whites deepen to gold.

  2. Swirl & smell

    Swirl to release aromas. Note fruit, flowers, spice, oak or earth. Smell is most of what we call taste.

  3. Sip

    Let it coat your mouth. Judge sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol (warmth) and body.

  4. Finish

    How long do the flavours last after swallowing? A long, pleasant finish usually signals quality.

Food pairing

Match weight with weight, and let acidity and sweetness balance the dish.

Sauvignon Blanc

Goat cheese, salads, seafood, herbs.

Chardonnay

Roast chicken, creamy pasta, lobster (oaked); oysters (unoaked).

Riesling

Spicy Asian dishes, pork, off-dry with heat.

Pinot Noir

Salmon, duck, mushrooms, roast chicken.

Cabernet Sauvignon

Steak, lamb, aged hard cheese.

Sparkling

Fried food, salty snacks, brunch, oysters.

Sangiovese

Tomato sauces, pizza, grilled meats.

Dessert wine

Blue cheese, foie gras, fruit tarts.

Storing

Keep bottles away from light and heat, at a steady 10–15 °C. Lay cork-sealed bottles on their side so the cork stays moist.

Opened bottles

Reseal and refrigerate. Whites and rosé last 3–5 days, reds 3–5 days, sparkling 1–3 days with a proper stopper.

Decanting

Young, tannic reds benefit from an hour or more of air. Old wines need only a gentle pour off the sediment.

Glassware

Use a tulip-shaped glass and fill about one third, leaving room to swirl.

Glossary

Acidity
The tart, mouth-watering freshness in wine.
Tannin
Compounds from skins, seeds and oak that create a drying, grippy feel.
Body
The weight of wine in the mouth: light, medium or full.
Dry / Off-dry / Sweet
Describes residual sugar, from almost none to noticeably sweet.
Vintage
The year the grapes were harvested.
Terroir
How soil, climate and place shape a wine’s character.
Appellation
A legally defined wine region with its own rules, e.g. AOC, DOC, AVA.
Brut
A dry style of sparkling wine.
Corked
Spoiled by TCA, giving a damp-cardboard smell. Not the same as bits of cork floating in the glass.
Sulfites
Preservatives present in nearly all wine, naturally and by addition.
ABV
Alcohol by volume. Table wine is usually 11–15%.
Sommelier
A trained wine professional, often in restaurants.
Drink responsibly. Wine is for adults of legal drinking age.
