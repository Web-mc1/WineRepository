Types of Wine

Wine is fermented grape juice. Colour, sweetness, and bubbles come from skin contact, secondary fermentation, and grape handling.

Red

Red Wine

Fermented with dark grape skins left in the juice, imparting rich color, tannins, and structural depth. Ranges from light Pinot Noir to robust Cabernet Sauvignon.

White

White Wine

Grapes are pressed prior to fermentation to separate juice from skins. Typically fresh with crisp acidity, ranging from zesty Sauvignon Blanc to buttery Chardonnay.

Rosé

Rosé Wine

Crafted from red grapes with brief skin maceration (hours instead of weeks), resulting in an elegant pink tint with bright berry and floral notes.

Sparkling

Sparkling Wine

Trapped carbon dioxide bubbles from secondary fermentation. Includes Champagne, Cava, Prosecco, and Crémant made via traditional or tank methods.

Sweet

Dessert Wine

Concentrated natural grape sugars left unfermented, often harvested late, frozen on the vine, or affected by noble rot (Sauternes, Tokaji, Ice Wine).

Fortified

Fortified Wine

Neutral grape spirits are added during or after fermentation to elevate alcohol content to 17–22% ABV. Includes Port, Sherry, Madeira, and Marsala.

How to Taste Wine

Follow the classic 4-step sommelier sensory evaluation technique.

01

Look

Tilt glass against a white surface. Clarity and hue hint at age and grape—reds fade to brick brown, whites deepen to golden amber.

02

Swirl & Smell

Swirl glass gently to release volatile aromatics. Inhale deeply to identify primary fruit notes, flowers, oak, spice, and earthiness.

03

Sip

Coat your palate. Evaluate structural components: sweetness level, mouth-watering acidity, drying tannin grip, alcohol warmth, and body.

04

Finish

Observe how long flavor impressions linger after swallowing. A lengthy, evolving finish directly correlates with premium wine quality.

Serving & Storage Guidelines

Optimal serving temperatures unlock proper fruit aromas and structural balance.

Temperature Chart

Sparkling & Sweet Whites6–8 °C (43–46 °F)
Light Whites & Rosé8–10 °C (46–50 °F)
Full-Bodied Whites10–12 °C (50–54 °F)
Light-Bodied Reds12–14 °C (54–57 °F)
Full-Bodied Reds15–18 °C (59–64 °F)

Care & Decanting

  • Storage: Lay corked bottles on their side at steady 10–15 °C away from light and vibration.
  • Opened Bottles: Re-cork and refrigerate. Whites & reds last 3–5 days; sparkling lasts 1–3 days.
  • Decanting: Aerate youthful tannic reds for 45–60 mins. Pour older vintage wines gently off sediment.

Wine Glossary

Essential terminology for discussing wine evaluation, winemaking, and flavors.

Acidity
The tart, mouth-watering freshness that balances fruit sweetness and alcohol.
Tannin
Astringent compounds from grape skins, seeds, and oak providing structural drying sensation.
Body
The tactile sensation of weight in the mouth: light, medium, or full.
Terroir
The complete natural environmental factors—soil, microclimate, and topography—defining a wine.
Appellation
A legally defined geographical wine region protected by official regulations (AOC, DOCG, AVA).
Vintage
The specific calendar year in which the grapes were harvested.
